# China's Guerrilla War (1927–1949): production plan and fact sheet (checkpoint 1)

The Major Revolution · episode 3 · target 11:45–12:00 · voice Kokoro bm_george (same as the Taiping episode)

**Scope.** "Chinese guerrilla movement" is told as the communist guerrilla war, from the Shanghai purge of 1927 to the founding of the People's Republic in 1949. It ends with how Mao's guerrilla rules spread abroad, including Nepal.

## Hook: three options (the script is written for A)
- **A. The army that ran away and won (recommended).** For 22 years Mao's army kept retreating, and then it took the most populous country on earth. How? *Working title: "The Army That Ran Away and Won China"*
- **B. Sixteen characters.** Sixteen Chinese characters beat an army four times bigger. *Working title: "16 Characters That Won China"*
- **C. Not one sweet potato.** Why a rule about stealing a sweet potato won a civil war. *Working title: "Mao's Strangest Weapon: Not Stealing"*

Switching to B or C means rewriting the cold open and the last chapter. The rest stays as it is.

## Spine device: the Sixteen Characters
The spine is Mao's real guerrilla formula, 敌进我退 · 敌驻我扰 · 敌疲我打 · 敌退我追: "The enemy advances, we retreat · halts, we harass · tires, we attack · retreats, we pursue."

It is a strip of 16 ink-brushed character tiles along the lower third, in four groups of four, each with a small English line under it.
- **Chapter 2:** the tiles are brushed in, one per word.
- **Each time a line is proven, its four tiles are stamped red:**
  - line 1 in chapter 3 (the first three campaigns, 1930–31)
  - line 2 in chapter 8 (behind Japanese lines)
  - line 3 in chapter 10 (Huaihai)
  - line 4 in chapter 11 (the Yangtze crossing, "All sixteen characters, complete")
- **Chapter 4:** under Braun, the four red tiles crack and grey.
- **Chapter 5:** at Zunyi, the cracks heal.
- **Chapter 10:** line 1 flashes again on "Mao simply walked away", when Yan'an is abandoned.
- **Chapter 12:** the 16 tiles lift off and fly across a world map to Vietnam, Peru and Nepal.

## Planted object: the sweet potato rule
- **Plant (about 1:40):** in October 1927, on the way to the mountains, Mao gives three rules. A single sweet potato sits on a hand-written rule sheet, with a red REMEMBER tag pinned to it. This is the first statement of the three-note "rule" motif.
- **Echo (about 2:30):** the sweet potato on the sheet is crossed out and replaced by a pinned needle and thread ("do not take even those").
- **Payoff (about 10:25, 88%):**
  1. On "Remember the sweet potato?" the same sheet, tag and motif return.
  2. Then comes the Shanghai pavement plate from the cold open: rows of sleeping soldiers at dawn, and shutters opening.
- **Counterweight:** chapter 12 opens on "But the promise had limits", which leads into the land reform killings.

## Content and sensitivity notes
- **Balance.** The film credits the guerrilla strategy but also shows its costs: the Futian/AB purge, the Changchun siege, the land reform killings and Mao's later rule.
- **No gore.**
  - The Shanghai 1927 purge, the Three Alls, Nanjing 1937 and Changchun are shown only as crimson-tinted silhouettes, smoke, empty streets and cuts to black.
  - Deaths are shown as medallions greying with a red slash.
- **Archival photos** show soldiers, leaders and events only, never victims.
- **Nepal.** The Nepal section is factual and neutral: the war, its death toll, the end of the monarchy, and Prachanda as PM.
