DESCRIPTION
In 1837, a young man from a Hakka village near Canton failed the imperial examinations, collapsed with fever, and saw visions of a golden-bearded old man who handed him a sword. Years later he decided the old man was God, and that he himself was the younger brother of Jesus Christ.

His name was Hong Xiuquan. His Taiping Heavenly Kingdom took Nanjing, ruled much of southern China for eleven years, banned opium and foot-binding, held its own examinations, tore itself apart in a purge, and finally fell in fire in 1864. By most estimates, the war killed 20 to 30 million people.

This is the story of the Taiping Rebellion: how a failed exam student built a kingdom of heaven, and why it ended in fire.

ACCURACY NOTES
- Death toll: most estimates are 20–30 million; some go much higher. The comparison with the First World War (about 15–22 million deaths, estimates) is given as "perhaps".
- Hong's vision is told as he later described it. God and Jesus are shown only as backlit silhouettes.
- Disputed details are hedged in the narration: the Confucian tablets, the 1856 purge (who ordered it and how many died), Anqing, the Taiping exams for women, and how Hong died (his son said illness; Li Xiucheng said poison).
- Zeng Guofan's report of 100,000 rebels killed at Nanjing may be exaggerated.
- The Taiping Land System (1853) was largely never carried out.
- The Chinese writing in the film is illustrative brush marks, not real characters.
