# Taiping Rebellion: production plan and fact sheet (checkpoint 1)

The Major Revolution · episode 2 · target 12:00 · voice Kokoro bm_george

## Hook: three options (the script is written for A)
- **A. The failed student (recommended).** He failed the imperial exam four times, decided he was the younger brother of Jesus, and started the deadliest civil war of the 19th century. *Working title: "The Failed Student Who Became God's Son"*
- **B. Deadlier than World War One.** How did a civil war most Westerners have never heard of kill 20–30 million people? *Working title: "The War That Killed More Than WWI"*
- **C. The Christian kingdom the West destroyed.** Why did Britain and France help a non-Christian dynasty crush a Christian revolution? *Working title: "China's Christian Revolution, and Why the West Crushed It"*

To switch to B or C, I'd rewrite the cold open and the last chapter. The body stays as it is.

## Spine device: the Heavenly Calendar
The Taiping really did issue their own calendar. The spine is a strip of 14 year-tiles (1851–1864) along the lower third. It appears at Jintian, and one tile is stamped red at each new year in the story. During the 1856 purge, cracks run through the tiles that are already stamped. On "About sixty yards of wall came down" the 14th tile shatters, and the strip burns on "the Heavenly Kingdom was gone." It plays the role the 1381 tally stick played.

## Planted object: the examination pass list
- **Plant (about 1:10):** a pass list is pasted on the Canton exam-hall wall, columns of names in ink. A finger runs down it; Hong's name isn't there. A red REMEMBER tag pins to the board. This is the first statement of the three-note "list" motif.
- **Echo 1 (about 4:05):** the Taiping hold their own exams, and Hong's name now heads the list as its examiner ("The man who had failed the exams now wrote them"). The motif plays in a major key.
- **Echo 2 (about 4:40):** Zeng Guofan, "who had passed every examination". His name sits at the top of a Qing list.
- **Payoff (about 10:15, 85%):** "remember the list…" The same board, tag and motif return. In 1905 the board is torn down with a red cancellation stamp. Then 1911–12: the Qing dragon flag falls (dip to black).

## Content and sensitivity notes
- Depicting God: in the vision, God and the elder brother are backlit silhouettes only. The narration always frames these as Hong's belief ("believed he was").
- No gore: the Nanjing massacre, the 1856 purge and Anqing are shown as crimson-tinted silhouettes, smoke and cuts to black, and deaths as medallions greying with a red slash.
